# Instance policy template

Copy this, fill it in, and publish it somewhere stable. Link it from the
instance. Reporters and publishers both look for it, and an instance that hosts
other people's work should say who is responsible for it.

Everything here is a starting point, not a legal document. Have a lawyer read
it if you are running something public.

## What this instance hosts

Describe the kinds of projects you will and will not carry. For example: game
mods, plugins, resource packs, and their metadata. Say what you exclude, such
as anything illegal where you operate, malware, or content that infringes
someone's rights.

## How moderation works

- Who reviews new projects, and whether admission is open or reviewed.
- What happens after a report: who sees it, roughly how long a first response
  takes, and what the reporter hears back.
- The outcomes you can choose from: take no action, add a warning, quarantine
  the project, delist it from search, block it from the directory, or block it
  from being served entirely.
- What each outcome does and does not do. Delisting and blocking change what
  this instance serves; they never rewrite a signed record, so a copy held
  elsewhere still verifies.

## Takedown requests

- Where to send one, and what it should contain: the project, the specific
  material, why you are entitled to ask, and how to reach you.
- How you verify a request before acting on it.
- What you do while you review: leave it up, quarantine it, or block it.
- How you tell the publisher, and whether they can respond.
- What you keep on record afterward.

## Data and retention

- What you store: signed metadata, uploaded artifacts, account details, and
  request logs.
- How long you keep staging uploads that were never published, unreferenced
  blobs, moderation records, and logs.
- Where the data lives and who can reach it.
- Whether you run backups, how often, and how someone asks about the data you
  hold about them.

## Security

- What you do with a reported vulnerability, and your expected timeline.
- How you handle signing keys and who has access to the instance.
- Whether this instance is covered by any external review.

## Changes

- How you announce a change to this policy.
- The date of the current version.
